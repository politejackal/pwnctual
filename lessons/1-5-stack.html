You can imagine a stack to be something like a box of books. You put something onto the stack, and it sits at the top of the stack. You put another thing, and now that new thing is at the top of the stack. When you remove something from the stack, you remove it from the top of the stack.

This gives the stack the LIFO rule, which means last-in-first-out. For example, the book you just placed on the stack will come out first, and the book which you placed first will come out last, after all the other books are taken out.

Each function has some space on the stack. This space is known as the stack frame for that function.
